This is the DC small-building market read I run for myself before I price one of my own properties. It is built for owners of 3 to 20 unit buildings, not for institutions and not for the citywide average. Every number below names its source.

Prepared by Shafiq Hirani, MBA, CCIM. Real estate advisor, owner, and broker of small multifamily in DC.

## The bottom line

DC buildings of 3 to 20 units now trade as two separate markets. The 3 to 4 unit class held its volume. The 5 to 20 unit class lost almost half its sales.

My closed comps show 163 buildings of 3 to 20 units sold in the 12 months to November 5, against 215 the year before. That is a 24% drop in count. Dollar volume fell 28%, from $272.2M to $195.7M.

The class split shows where that drop sits:

1. 3 to 4 units: 117 buildings sold, against 127. Count down 8%. Volume down 8%, to $119.2M from $129.1M.
2. 5 to 20 units: 46 buildings sold, against 89. Count down 48%. Volume down 47%, to $76.5M from $144.8M.

The 5 to 20 class carries most of the blended volume drop. On a quality-adjusted basis, values are flat. They have not recovered.

## The value read

My blended median closed price is $197,500 per unit, up 2.6% from $192,500. That blend hides two price levels, and neither one is $197,500.

1. 3 to 4 units: median $219,000 per unit on 117 sales, against $217,500 the year before.
2. 5 to 20 units: median $135,000 per unit on 46 sales, against $121,875.

Do not read the blended rise as a recovery. The raw median moves with the mix of buildings that happen to sell in a given year. My quality-adjusted index strips out that mix, and it reads flat.

In my closed comps, the sales that did close behaved like this:

1. Median sale to asking: 100.0% in 3 to 4 unit buildings (prior 95.46%) and 98.85% in 5 to 20 (prior 93.03%). Blended, 99.67% against 95.08%.
2. Median days on market: 71.5 in 3 to 4 (prior 63) and 261 in 5 to 20 (prior 260). Blended, 83.5 against 98.
3. Median reported cap rate: 6.9% on 15 sales in 3 to 4 (prior 7.26% on 15) and 7.0% on 9 sales in 5 to 20 (prior 6.96% on 14). Blended, 6.95% on 24 sales. These samples are thin, so treat them as a direction only.

## Supply and listings

New small-building supply is thin, and all of it sits in the larger class.

1. Units under construction: 0 in 3 to 4 unit buildings (prior 7) and 172 in 5 to 20 (prior 188).
2. For-sale listings: 53 in 3 to 4, covering 242 units, and 78 in 5 to 20, covering 1,082 units. CoStar's blended run shows 119 listings and 1,282 units, because the class counts overlap.
3. Asking price per unit: $323,544 in 3 to 4 and $172,591 in 5 to 20.
4. Vacant units: 418 in 3 to 4 and 1,668 in 5 to 20.

The 5 to 20 class has 78 listings on the market against 44 transactions in the past year on CoStar's count, alongside 11.5 months to sale.

## What it means for you

Run one check before you talk to any lender. Use your own class, never the blended $197,500.

1. Multiply your unit count by $219,000 if you own a 3 to 4 unit building, or by $135,000 if you own a 5 to 20 unit building.
2. Compare the result to your loan balance.
3. If your loan matures in the next 18 months, book a broker opinion of value this month.

Here is the math on a six-unit building, which sits in the 5 to 20 class. Six units at $135,000 per unit is $810,000. That figure is illustrative median math only. Your submarket and your rent roll will move it.

The median 5 to 20 unit sale was a 12-unit building, and the median 3 to 4 unit sale was a 4-unit building.

Fewer recent comps now sit behind any appraisal, and an owner of a 5 to 20 unit building faces the thinnest set. Know your number before the bank orders its own.
