This is the DC small-building market read I run for myself before I price one of my own properties. It is built for owners of 4 to 20 unit buildings, not for institutions and not for the citywide average. Every number below names its source.

Prepared by Shafiq Hirani, MBA, CCIM. Real estate advisor, owner, and broker of small multifamily in DC.

## The bottom line

Small DC apartment building values have stabilised. They have not recovered. The weak spot is volume.

My own closed comps count 186 buildings of 1 to 20 units sold in the 12 months to 5 November. The year before, 258 sold. Dollar volume fell about 30%, from $304.6M to $212.8M.

## The value read

The median sale price is $206,250 per unit, against $200,000 in the prior 12 months. On paper, that is a 3.1% rise.

Do not read that as a recovery. The raw median moves with the mix of buildings that happen to sell in a given year. My quality-adjusted index for 3 to 20 unit buildings strips out that mix, and it reads flat.

1. The mix-adjusted median was $179,946 per unit in Q3 2026, against $180,826 in Q3 2025, down 0.5%.
2. The smoothed index rose 0.2% year over year in Q3, on 36 sales. Both measures sit within half a percent of zero.
3. It fell 2.2% in Q2.
4. The partial Q4 reads down 1.9%, on only 12 sales.

The raw median rose. The quality-adjusted index is flat. The right word is stable.

October's median of $266,471 per unit came from just 16 sales. That sample is too thin to call a trend.

The sales that did close moved faster and closer to ask. Median days on market fell to 79 from 95.5. The median sale closed at 98.3% of asking price, up from 94.7%. The median deal was a 4-unit building.

My own comps show a reported cap rate median of 6.95% on 24 reported sales, against 6.96% the year before. That sample is thin, so treat it as a direction only.

## Supply and listings

New small-building supply is thin. CoStar counts 172 units under construction in DC buildings of 0 to 20 units, against 163 in September and an inventory of 26,850 units across 3,300 buildings.

The 4-plus unit market has 6,318 units under construction and absorbed 3,855 units over the past 12 months. At most 172 of those units sit in buildings of 20 units or fewer. The rest is larger product.

On the selling side, CoStar tracks 195 DC multifamily listings for sale as of 10 November. Of those, 159 are active, 34 are under contract and 2 are in escrow. CoStar's own sale comps for 0 to 20 unit buildings show 85 sales so far in 2026, covering 625 units and $120M, with a median cap rate of 6.8% on 14 reported sales.

## What it means for you

Run one check before you talk to any lender.

1. Multiply your unit count by $206,250.
2. Compare the result to your loan balance.
3. If your loan matures in the next 18 months, book a broker opinion of value this month.

Here is the math on a six-unit building. Six units at $206,250 per unit is $1,237,500. That figure is illustrative median math. Your submarket and your rent roll will move it.

With 28% fewer sales than the year before, fewer recent comps sit behind any appraisal. Know your number before the bank orders its own.
