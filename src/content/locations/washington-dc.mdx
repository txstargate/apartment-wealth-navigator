Sixty apartment buildings of 4 to 20 units sold in Washington DC from January through July 2026. They sold at a median of $200,000 per unit, across a range from about $63,000 to $550,000 per unit. That spread is why a citywide average tells an owner almost nothing about a single building.

Each section below links to the full guide behind it.

## The DC market in four numbers

- **Price per unit.** A median of $200,000 across 60 sales of 4- to 20-unit buildings, January through July 2026.
- **Cap rate.** Of 314 recorded sales from 2024 through July 2026, only 43 reported a cap rate. The median sat near 6.25 percent, with the middle half between about 5.0 and 7.7 percent.
- **Vacancy.** About 9.4 percent, up from 8.7 percent a year earlier.
- **Direction.** The median price per unit for 4- to 7-unit buildings fell about 7 percent over the past 12 months, from $206,750 to $191,894.

## What moves a DC building's value

- **Building class.** Class B buildings sold at a median of $244,748 per unit. Class C sold at $191,250, a gap of about 28 percent.
- **Renovation.** Renovated buildings sold at a median of $231,250 per unit, against $198,750 for buildings with no recorded renovation. That is about a 16 percent difference.
- **Net operating income.** At a 6.25 percent cap rate, every recurring dollar of NOI adds about $16 of value. Recover $10,000 a year, and the building is worth roughly $160,000 more.

With vacancy rising and rent stabilization limiting covered rents, the expense side is where a DC owner can still add value. ## Three DC rules that change the number

**The Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act (TOPA).** When you sell a covered building, tenants hold a right to purchase first. That adds time and process to a DC sale that a Virginia sale does not carry. Some Maryland counties now have rent control legislation, so the county matters there. Buyers price that timing risk in. Planning the TOPA sequence early protects both your price and your closing date. Read [Selling a DC Apartment Building: TOPA Timing](/insights/selling-dc-apartment-building-topa/)

**Rent stabilization.** Many older DC buildings sit under the District's rent-control rules, which limit how fast rents on covered units can rise. If your rents sit below market in a covered building, you cannot close that gap overnight, and a buyer knows it. That caps the optimistic end of your value range.

**Lender expectations.** A DC lender takes the lower of two tests: about 75 percent of appraised value, and the loan that keeps NOI at roughly 1.25 times the mortgage payment. On small DC buildings the coverage test usually controls, so your defensible NOI decides the number.
