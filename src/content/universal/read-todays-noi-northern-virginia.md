# How to Read Today's NOI on a Northern Virginia Apartment Building

## How do you read today's NOI when higher rates have repriced Northern Virginia apartments?

Build the NOI from what the building produces now: the current rent roll, the executed leases and the trailing twelve months. Then test it against today's financing. Higher rates shrink the loan a lender will write on the same income, and the price a buyer can pay moves with that loan.

## Key takeaways

- Northmarq put Northern Virginia apartment vacancy at 4.7% in the third quarter of 2026, up 70 basis points from a year earlier. A pro forma that assumes full occupancy starts too high.
- Northern Virginia asking rents averaged $2,341 per month in the same quarter, down 0.4% year over year. That figure never replaces your own rent roll.
- A lender sizes your loan from its own NOI and its required debt service coverage ratio. A higher rate means a smaller loan on the same income.
- The $297,800 per unit year-to-date median for the Washington area includes larger institutional trades. Do not apply it to a 5 to 50 unit building.

## Why read today's NOI before you read any asking price?

Buyers and lenders do not see your price logic. They see documents. You formed your earlier price expectation under earlier assumptions. It does not change what your leases and operating statement show today.

Whoever lends against your building next, and whoever buys it, will value what it earns now and check that figure against paper. I walk owners through this before a listing, a refinance or an offer. Find the gap between the number in your head and the number on paper before an underwriter does.

Northmarq reported Northern Virginia vacancy of 4.7% in the third quarter of 2026. That is a market number. Your physical vacancy, economic vacancy, lease expirations and collection loss come from your building's records.

### What records should the NOI come from?

The current rent roll, the executed leases and the trailing twelve-month operating statement, checked against bank deposits. For a 5 to 50 unit building, I want the full file:

- Current rent roll and executed leases
- Trailing twelve-month operating statement
- Bank statements for the building
- Utility bills and real estate tax bills
- Insurance declarations
- Service contracts and repair invoices
- Documentation for recurring capital items

A rent strategy, an expense cut or a price opinion built on a summary instead of the source documents is a guess.

### How do you calculate NOI on a 5 to 50 unit building?

Start with scheduled rent. Subtract vacancy and collection loss. Add other income. Subtract operating expenses. Stop there. Debt service, depreciation, income taxes and capital expenditures are not operating expenses.

Three places owners make mistakes:

- **Physical versus economic vacancy.** An occupied unit can still produce less than scheduled rent because of concessions, delinquency, repayment plans, employee units or below-market legacy leases. Build NOI from collected rent, not advertised rent.
- **Rent roll versus market rents.** In-place NOI uses the rent roll. Market rents are upside. A buyer treats them as your plan, not as income they can verify.
- **Current versus stabilized NOI.** A cap rate applies to NOI you can document. A stabilized projection is a forecast, and buyers pay for it only after they discount the risk of getting there.

Do not capitalize gross rent. It overstates the building every time, and a lender takes it apart in the first week.

## How do higher interest rates change what an apartment building is worth?

They cut the loan a lender will write against the same NOI, so the same income supports a lower price. The lender applies its required coverage ratio to its own NOI, and the maximum loan falls out of the payment that NOI can carry.

### What does the arithmetic look like?

Here is a hypothetical, not a quote. A lender verifies $300,000 of NOI and requires a 1.25 coverage ratio. Divide $300,000 by 1.25 and the building can carry $240,000 of annual debt service. Assume a 30-year amortization with monthly payments, and change only the rate:

| Rate | Supportable loan |
| --- | --- |
| 5.5% | about $3.52 million |
| 6.0% | about $3.34 million |
| 6.5% | about $3.16 million |

That is roughly 10% less loan between 5.5% and 6.5%, and the building did not change. A buyer who priced the deal on the old loan must bring the difference in equity or ask for a lower price. Real quotes also depend on loan-to-value limits, term, reserves, sponsorship, condition and lender type. Your lender sets the actual figures.

The Federal Reserve's [selected interest rates release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/) publishes daily market rates. It describes the environment. Your loan rate comes from your lender's quote. I cover the lender side in [The Refinance Math Lenders Don't Tell Apartment Owners](/insights/refinance-math-lenders-dont-tell-apartment-owners/).

### Does strong deal volume mean your price expectation holds?

No. Northmarq counted 16 Washington-area multifamily trades for $1.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Year-to-date volume of $4.2 billion already passed the full 2025 total. Northern Virginia accounted for 70% of that dollar volume, and three Arlington and Alexandria trades each priced above $475,000 per unit.

Those trades show capital is active and that location and quality earn a premium. They do not set the price of a 5 to 50 unit building with its own rent roll, expense history and deferred items. Your buyer will still run your documented income through a lender's formula.

## What should you do with your NOI before a sale, refinance or offer?

Rebuild it from documents, stress it against current financing and fix what you can before a buyer's lender sees it.

- **Build in-place NOI from the records.** Use collected rent, actual vacancy and collection loss, and trailing twelve-month expenses. Tie every line to a bill, invoice or deposit.
- **Run a lower-income case.** Assume weaker collections or slower lease-up and see whether the loan still works.
- **Size the loan the way a lender will.** Use a current quote with a stated coverage ratio, rate, amortization and loan-to-value limit.
- **Compare the loan to your price.** The gap is the equity a buyer must bring or the cut a seller must absorb.
- **Improve what you can document.** My posts on [increasing NOI on a small apartment building](/insights/how-to-increase-noi-small-apartment-building/) and [revenue capture](/insights/revenue-capture-recover-noi-without-raising-rent/) show where owners usually find money without waiting on the market.

Buyers can run the same file in reverse. Test the offering memorandum against the seller's own records, and let the lender's NOI set your budget.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do higher interest rates change the value of a Northern Virginia apartment building?

They lower the loan a lender will write against a given NOI, which lowers the price a buyer can pay without adding equity. The lender applies its required debt service coverage ratio to its own NOI, so a higher rate means a smaller supportable loan. The size of the effect depends on loan-to-value limits, term, amortization and lender type.

### What expenses should I subtract to calculate NOI on a 5 to 50 unit property?

Subtract operating expenses such as real estate taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs and maintenance, service contracts and management. Do not subtract debt service, depreciation or income taxes. Show capital expenditures and reserves on their own lines.

### Should I use the current rent roll or market rents when underwriting NOI?

Use the current rent roll for in-place NOI. Market rents belong in a separate upside case, because a lender and a buyer can verify leases but not the increase you expect. Regional rent data is context for that case, not a replacement for your leases.

### What debt service coverage ratio do lenders look for on a small Northern Virginia apartment building?

Lenders commonly look for a ratio in the range of 1.20 to 1.25, but the requirement varies by lender, loan-to-value and loan type. Ask the lender quoting your loan for its required ratio and how it calculates NOI. That answer drives the loan amount more than the asking price does.

### Can an earlier price expectation still hold if current NOI is lower?

Only if a buyer and a lender can verify how the gap closes, such as signed leases at higher rents or documented expense cuts. An expectation formed under earlier assumptions is an opinion. Current records, current financing and the buyer's required return decide what the building supports today.
