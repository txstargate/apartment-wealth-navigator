Over 30 years in this business, I have watched owners decide to sell about 18 months later than they should have. That delay is almost never a market problem. It is a signal problem.

They were watching the wrong things. Meanwhile three clocks kept running: the loan clock, the capital expense clock, and the tax clock. None of them wait for you to feel ready.

## The trap is waiting for a feeling

Owners wait to feel ready. They wait for the market to feel better. They wait for a number on the news to tell them it is finally time.

By the time the feeling shows up, the advantage has moved to the buyer. You are selling on his timeline, into his questions, with a loan maturity sitting behind you that he reads as clearly as you do.

The market did not fail you there. Set-and-forget ownership caught up at the worst possible moment.

## Five signals that say sell

Replace the feeling with signals. Any one of these five puts your building in play.

1. **A loan maturing inside 24 months.** That is not a future problem. It is a today problem, because your options shrink every month you wait.
2. **A capital expense wall you cannot pay for out of cash flow.** Roof, boiler, and windows all landing in the same window. If the building cannot fund its own repairs, you are about to fund them personally.
3. **An NOI ceiling.** The point where the next dollar of income costs you more than a dollar to go get. You have taken the easy wins. What is left is expensive.
4. **A wide spread between what you paid and what the building is worth today.** That is real equity sitting still, doing nothing for you.
5. **A partnership change or a life event that shortens your hold.** A partner who wants out, a health issue, a move. The math changes whether you like it or not.

These line up with the triggers in [How to Prepare a Small Apartment Building for Sale](/insights/how-to-prepare-apartment-building-for-sale/), which covers the work that starts once a signal fires.

## Three signals that say wait

1. **Unrecovered NOI leaks sitting in your operating statement.** Every dollar in there is a dollar the buyer keeps instead of you.
2. **Rents that are mid-repositioning.** You did the work, but a trailing 12-month statement does not show it yet. Sell now and you are selling on old numbers.
3. **A prepayment penalty that has not aged out.** Sometimes waiting six months is worth six figures.

## Waiting on purpose is not the same as waiting by default

Waiting with a fix already in motion is a strategy. Waiting because you have not looked at your own numbers in three years is what quietly costs you.

If you are waiting for the market to feel better, you have already made your decision. You just have not admitted it yet.

Selling is not a market call. It is a readiness call. Nobody times the cap rate. You can time your own income statement, and that is the half of the equation you control.

## What the readiness half is worth

Take a building carrying $20,000 a year in unrecovered NOI. Expenses that should not be there, income that is not being captured. Take it to market anyway.

At a 6 percent cap rate the arithmetic is $20,000 divided by 0.06, or $333,333.

That building sells for roughly $330,000 less than it should have. That is a preparation discount rather than a market discount, and the buyer keeps every dollar of it.

An owner with nine units called me three months out from listing, ready to go. We ran the audit first instead. Six months later he had recovered $24,000 in annual NOI. Same building, same market, same month of the year.

The arithmetic: $24,000 divided by 0.06 is $400,000.

That is roughly $400,000 of added value from work that took two quarters, and it changed his decision entirely.

## Run the number on your own building tonight

Take your annual NOI and divide it by 0.06. That is roughly what a buyer is pricing. Then ask yourself how much of that number you have never actually gone looking for.

**When should I sell my apartment building?**
When the signals say so, not when the market feels right. A loan maturing inside 24 months, a capital expense wall you cannot fund from cash flow, an NOI ceiling, a wide spread between what you paid and today's value, or a partnership change or life event that shortens your hold.

**What are the signals that say I should wait to sell?**
Three. Unrecovered NOI leaks still sitting in your operating statement, rents that are mid-repositioning and not yet showing in a trailing 12-month statement, and a prepayment penalty that has not aged out. Waiting six months is sometimes worth six figures.

**How much does unrecovered NOI cost me at sale?**
