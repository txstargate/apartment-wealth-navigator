Owners fix what they can see. The process that keeps the problem from coming back is the part that never gets installed.

That is why an NOI gain has a shelf life. Billing discipline decays during a staffing change. A vendor add-on appears and nobody signs off on it. A lease expires with 30 days of warning instead of 90. The next water anomaly runs four months before someone reads the bill closely.

None of it is dramatic. No single month looks wrong. That is exactly how it survives.

The owners who hold an NOI improvement are not the ones who made the biggest initial fix. They are the ones who installed the systems that kept the fix working.

## Drift is a detection problem

Three blind spots create it.

No regular reporting means no early warning when a number moves. No renewal calendar means leases expire and you scramble instead of planning. No KPI review means you do not see a trend until it has already cost you.

All three share one trait. In every case the information existed. The water usage was printed on a bill. The expiration date was written in a lease. The turn time was sitting in a maintenance log. Nobody put the information in front of a person on a schedule.

One system closes each gap. Area five of the [NOI Leak Check](/tools/noi-check/) is the systems audit, and it is the area I find missing most often.

## System 1: The monthly reporting package

Every month, four documents should arrive without you asking for them.

1. **A rent roll.** Every unit, contract rent, lease end date, and current balance.
2. **A profit and loss statement.** Month, year to date, and against budget.
3. **A variance report.** Every line that moved, with a written reason attached.
4. **A forward action list.** What gets done next month, by whom, by when.

The variance report is the one owners skip and the one that catches drift. A profit and loss statement tells you water cost more this month. A variance report tells you it cost more than the same month last year and that nobody knows why. The second version starts a plumber call. The first version gets filed.

If you are not receiving all four, you are managing blind. That is not a criticism of your manager. It is a description of what you are allowed to see.

## System 2: The renewal calendar

Track every lease expiration 90 to 120 days out, not 30.

What fails here is the calendar, not the argument. An owner who agrees with the 90-day rule and has no column tracking expiration dates is still finding out at 30.

The calendar itself is one column added to the rent roll and one recurring reminder. The discipline is checking it on the same day every month, whether or not anything expires inside the window.

## System 3: The KPI dashboard

Four numbers, monthly, each compared to the prior month and the prior year.

1. **Occupancy rate.**
2. **Average turn time**, measured move-out to move-in.
3. **Utility cost per unit.**
4. **Delinquency rate.**

Two comparisons, not one. Month over month catches a sudden break. Year over year catches slow drift, and slow drift is the failure mode that takes the money. A utility cost per unit that rises 2 percent a month looks acceptable on every monthly comparison. It finishes the year more than 25 percent above where it started.

Keep it to one page. A dashboard nobody reads is the same as no dashboard.

## What 30 minutes a month is protecting

Take a building where operational fixes recovered $10,000 a year of NOI. That is what the first four levers produce when you work all of them.

Reviewing the monthly package takes about 30 minutes. Twelve months of that is six hours a year.

Now price what those six hours defend. Recurring NOI is capitalized when you refinance or sell. At a 5 to 7 percent cap rate, $10,000 of recovered NOI is worth roughly $140,000 to $200,000 of building value. Six hours a year is what stands between that and a slow return to where you started.

It is the best-paid 30 minutes in your month. It is also the first thing owners give away, because nothing appears to be wrong.

## What to ask for this week

Three requests, one email.

1. Send the monthly package with all four documents, by a fixed date, starting this month.
2. Add lease expiration dates to the rent roll and flag everything inside 120 days.
3. Report the four KPIs each month with the prior month and the prior year beside them.

None of it costs money. All of it is reporting you are entitled to ask for. A manager who cannot produce it has just told you how he runs the building.
