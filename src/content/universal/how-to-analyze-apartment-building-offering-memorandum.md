# How to Analyze an Apartment Building Offering Memorandum

## How do you analyze an apartment building offering memorandum?

Rebuild the NOI yourself from records, using a lender's assumptions, before you accept the asking price. The offering memorandum shows a projection. You will own the building's actual income and expenses. I call the rebuild a stress test, and it has three moves.

## Key takeaways

- A pro forma is a projection built to support the asking price. It is not a record of what the building earned.
- At a 6% cap rate, every dollar of NOI that is not real adds about $16.67 to your price.
- On a 12-unit example, three adjustments cut NOI from $168,000 to $146,000. That is a $367,000 price difference.
- Lenders size your loan from the real NOI. Plan financing around the pro forma and you bring more cash to closing.

The listing's numbers say this building makes $168,000 a year. The real number is $146,000. That $22,000 gap looks small. Run it through a 6% cap rate and it becomes about $367,000. That is what you would overpay on day one, before you collect a single month of rent.

## Why is the pro forma in an offering memorandum optimistic?

Every offering memorandum comes with a pro forma. A pro forma is a projection of what the building will earn. The listing agent usually prepares it with the owner's input. It is a marketing document built to support the asking price.

That does not make anyone dishonest. It makes the pro forma optimistic by design. Three choices show up again and again:

- Rents show at market rate instead of what tenants pay today.
- Vacancy shows at 2% because the building happened to be full last year.
- The owner's own time managing the property never appears as a cost.

Each choice sounds reasonable. Each one supports the price.

## Why does a small NOI gap become a large price gap?

Price follows NOI, net operating income. NOI is what remains after operating expenses and before the mortgage. A cap rate is the yield a buyer expects from that income. You pay for NOI at a cap rate.

At a 6% cap rate, every dollar of income that is not real adds about $16.67 to your price. For an owner like you, the cash going into the building is the money meant to become your family's wealth. Overpaying spends part of it on day one.

## What are the three moves of a stress test?

The fix is simple to describe and takes discipline to do. You rebuild the numbers from records, using the assumptions a lender will use.

### Move one: replace projections with records

Ask for the T12, the trailing 12 months of income and expenses. Ask for the current rent roll. Use the rents tenants pay today, not the rents the pro forma hopes for.

### Move two: use a lender's assumptions

Lenders generally take the greater of actual or market vacancy, or a policy floor. Common floors are 5% for Fannie Mae multifamily and effectively 7% for HUD market-rate multifamily, which caps economic occupancy at 93%. Other property types follow lender-specific floors or market norms. Lenders also include a management fee, typically 5% to 6% depending on the market, even if the owner self-manages. You will either hire a manager or pay with your own time, usually at night. They also expect replacement reserves for future repairs.

### Move three: reprice every expense as if you already own the building

- Get a current insurance quote.
- Ask how property taxes could change after a sale.
- Confirm who pays which utilities.

## What does a stress test look like on a 12-unit building?

Here is the example from the video. The building has 12 units and an asking price of $2.8 million. The pro forma NOI is $168,000, which is a 6% cap rate. It looks fair. Now stress it.

| Line | Adjustment | NOI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pro forma NOI | | $168,000 |
| Vacancy from 2% to 5% | minus $8,000 | $160,000 |
| Management fee the pro forma left out | minus $10,000 | $150,000 |
| Insurance updated to a current quote | minus $4,000 | $146,000 |

The real NOI is $146,000. At the same $2.8 million price, your cap rate falls to about 5.2%. To buy at a true 6%, the price is closer to $2.43 million.

Same building, same tenants. The difference is $367,000. You found it on paper, before you spent a dollar on due diligence. ## How does a lower NOI affect your loan?

The damage does not stop at price. Lenders size your loan from the real NOI, not the pro forma's version. Plan your financing around $168,000, get underwritten at $146,000, and your loan shrinks. You bring more cash to closing.

## What is the 5-minute test you can run tonight?

Run this on the offering memorandum you already have open. It takes 5 minutes.

1. Take the pro forma NOI.
2. Cut it by 10%.
3. Divide the result by the asking price.

On the example, $168,000 cut by 10% is $151,200. Divided by $2.8 million, that is 5.4%. If the deal only works at the pro forma number, it does not work.

## What happened when I stress tested a six-unit DC building for Rob?

Rob already owned a three-unit building in Virginia. He asked me, as his real estate advisor and broker, to look at a six-unit rent-controlled building in DC.

The listing projections looked strong. However, there was no current rent roll and no reliable operating statement. When we finally got the actual records, the advertised return only worked if rents rose about 140% while expenses fell 30%.

The projected rents were more than double what comparable buildings supported. The building was also rent-controlled and carried serious deferred maintenance.

Rob was cautious at first. He did not know me well yet, and he assumed the marketing materials had been prepared with care. So I did not ask him to trust me. I showed him the income, the expenses, the comps, the condition and the rent control limits.

He walked away. His capital is still there for the right building. That is what a stress test is for.
