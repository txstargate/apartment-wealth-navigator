Two owners with nearly identical buildings walk out with very different loans. The difference is almost never the property. It is what each one handed the lender before the lender had to ask.

An underwriter resolves every uncertainty in your file conservatively. That is the job. So the work is not producing a polished document. The work is leaving nothing to guess.

## Every gap in the file gets filled with a conservative number

A lender does not leave a blank line blank. An unexplained vacancy spike becomes a higher vacancy assumption. An expense year nobody accounts for becomes a padded expense line. A rent roll that disagrees with the income statement makes both documents useless, because the underwriter now has no idea which one is true.

None of that is hostility. It is what an underwriter does with missing information.

Take the 12-unit building from [How to Refinance a Small Apartment Building](/insights/how-to-refinance-small-apartment-building/), with $480,000 in gross rents. The standard vacancy assumption is 5 percent. On a file with an unexplained vacancy quarter, that assumption drifts to 8 percent.

Three percentage points of $480,000 is $14,400 of income. At roughly $10,000 of loan per $1,000 of defensible income, that drift costs about $144,000 in proceeds. The arithmetic: $14,400 times ten.

That is one assumption, moving three points, because of one unexplained quarter.

## The four buckets

Build the file in four parts. Each one answers a question the underwriter is already asking.

1. **The financial package.** A clean trailing twelve months, a current rent roll, and the last two years of operating statements. The numbers across those three documents have to agree. If your rent roll and your income statement disagree, the lender trusts neither.
2. **The operations snapshot.** Your actual vacancy and your actual expenses, plus a short written explanation of anything unusual. The water leak that spiked one quarter. The unit that turned over twice. You explain it before they wonder about it.
3. **The property overview.** Recent capital improvements, the condition of the major systems, and anything you have invested that supports value. A new roof belongs in the file, with the invoice and the date.
4. **The owner narrative.** One page. Who you are, how long you have held the building, what your plan is. It reads soft, and it does real work: it tells the lender they are dealing with a competent operator, and that lowers their sense of risk.

## The written explanation is the highest-value page in the file

Bucket two carries more weight than its size suggests. An underwriter who finds an unexplained expense spike has to assume it repeats. An underwriter who finds two sentences and a plumber's invoice can treat it as an event that already happened and got fixed.

Same number. Two very different treatments.

So go through your trailing twelve and find every line that would make a stranger pause. Write two sentences next to each one. Attach the invoice, the inspection report, or the dated photo. That is the entire technique.

Pick the one number in your file you would least want a lender questioning. That is exactly the number to explain up front.

## What a weekend of paperwork is worth

When the lender can verify every figure and every irregularity is already accounted for, there is no reason left to apply the conservative assumption. The vacancy factor stays where it belongs. The expense line does not get padded. The management fee is the only adjustment left standing, and that one is coming whether you self-manage or not.

Run the multiplier on the difference. Protect $14,400 of income with documentation and you protect roughly $144,000 of loan proceeds. Protect the full $38,400 the underwriter removed from the example building and the number reaches $384,000.

Nothing else you do on the building pays like that weekend of paperwork.

## Frequently asked questions

**What documents does a lender need to refinance an apartment building?**
Four buckets. A financial package of trailing twelve months, current rent roll, and two years of operating statements that agree. An operations snapshot of real vacancy and real expenses. A property overview of capital work and system condition. A one-page owner narrative.

**Does a better loan package actually change my loan amount?**
Yes, through the income the underwriter uses. Every $1,000 of income you protect from a conservative assumption is worth roughly $10,000 of borrowing capacity. Three points of unexplained vacancy on $480,000 of gross rents costs about $144,000 of proceeds.

**Should I explain a bad quarter or hope the lender misses it?**
Explain it. An underwriter who finds an unexplained spike assumes it repeats and prices for that. A dated invoice and two sentences turn a pattern back into an event, and events do not get built into a vacancy or expense assumption.
