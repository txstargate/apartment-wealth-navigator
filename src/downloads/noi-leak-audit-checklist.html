The NOI Leak Audit

One page. About 20 minutes.
Have your last 12 months of statements, the rent roll, and the utility bills in front of you.

Five levers raise net operating income on a small apartment building. In any given building two of them are usually carrying the loss, and the expensive part is guessing which two. One question per lever. Tick the answer you can prove from your own records.

LeverThe questionYour answer
1Revenue capture Are all lease-permitted fees billed consistently, every month, on every unit? Late fees, pet rent, parking, storage, utility recovery. Yes, and I can show the ledgerSome months, some unitsI do not know
2Utilities Have water or sewer bills risen more than 15 percent in any three consecutive months over the last 12? A rise that never reverses is a leak or a billing error. No, checked bill by billYes, and it never came downI have not compared them
3Vendors When were your top five contracts last competitively rebid? Landscaping, trash, cleaning, elevator or boiler service, insurance. Within the last two yearsThree years or moreThey auto-renew, I do not know
4Turns What is your average time from move-out to move-in, and who is tracking lease expirations 90 to 120 days out? Under 30 days, tracked30 to 60 daysOver 60, or nobody tracks it
5Systems Do you receive a monthly rent roll, profit and loss statement, and variance report, and do you read them within the week? All three, every monthSome of them, some monthsOnly when I ask

The rule: any question you cannot answer from your own records inside five minutes is already a finding. The missing answer tells you which lever to open first. Two or more answers in the bottom row means the building has a gap worth measuring before your next refinance or sale.
